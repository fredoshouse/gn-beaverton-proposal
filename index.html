Executive Summary

A race people
come back to.

The opportunity is to build a brand foundation strong enough to launch one event and flexible enough to carry the series that follows, done properly the first time so nothing has to be rebuilt.

Most run series begin as a logistics project. Pick a date, map a course, print a bib. The ones that last begin as a brand. A feeling people want to be part of, a reason to come back next year and bring someone with them.

Centering this on mothers isn't a demographic choice. It's a positioning choice, and it's the strongest thing you have. It means the brand can say something no generic community 5K can say. The work is to find that thing and say it clearly, before anyone designs a logo.

We'd approach this as a phased engagement spanning strategy, storytelling, identity, web, and print, sequenced around what you actually need at each stage and priced so you spend when it counts.

"Get the story right, and the flyer, the site, and the second event all get easier."

You asked us to recommend the right scope and pricing rather than execute against a spec. We've taken that seriously, and responded with a real point of view on what it takes to build this well.

The result is one engagement: the full foundation and launch, both phases, delivered as a single continuous run. It begins with the strategy phase, the asset everything else is built on, and carries through to a live, registerable brand well ahead of June 2027. One senior team, one timeline, one price.

The result should be a brand that feels made for the mothers of Beaverton, works on race day, and still holds up at event number four.

You're building an audience from zero with the first event eleven months out. The brand has to earn attention before it can earn a registration, which means the story and the identity have to be right before any of it goes public.

Key Assumptions & Scope Notes

Setting the project
up to move.

These assumptions shape both the cost and the timeline. Being clear about them up front protects everyone and keeps the engagement running well.

Key Assumptions
  • You designate a single decision-maker for consolidated approvals at each milestone
  • Feedback is consolidated at each review, not delivered on a rolling basis
  • Registration platform selection confirmed before the web build begins
  • Any existing materials or research shared ahead of discovery
  • Phase 2 begins within a reasonable window of Phase 1 close, or is re-scoped
  • Fees are based on the hours required per phase; work beyond agreed scope would be discussed and approved first
  • Delays in assets, feedback, or approvals will move final delivery accordingly
Out of Scope Unless Added

To keep expectations clear, the following are not assumed to be included unless explicitly scoped:

  • Media buying, paid placement, or ad spend
  • Photography, video production, or original illustration
  • Merchandise design, apparel, or race-day environmental build
  • Registration platform fees or third-party software costs
  • Print production and vendor management
  • Ongoing site maintenance beyond handoff and training
  • Full copywriting across all pages beyond core brand copy
  • Travel expenses
